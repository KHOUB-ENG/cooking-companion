# Recipe Photo Brief — 38 images

Paste this whole file into your image AI. It needs to produce **38 images**, all
in one consistent style, named exactly as specified.

---

## Read this part first

**Consistency matters more than any individual image.** These appear one after
another in a swipe deck, so the eye compares them directly. A single photo shot
from a different angle or on a different surface looks like a mistake. Every
image must share the same camera angle, the same surface, the same light, and
the same mood.

**Output format for every image:**

- **Aspect ratio: 1:1 (square).** Not 4:3, not 16:9, not portrait.
- **Resolution: around 2048 × 2048** (or 1024 × 1024 — either is plenty)
- **JPG**
- Named exactly as the filename given for each one (e.g. `chilli.jpg`)

### Why square, and what it means for composition

The app crops these to fill a fixed box, and that box is a different shape
depending on the screen:

| Where | Visible box | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone (the main case) | 320 × 303 | 1.06 — near square |
| Laptop | 444 × 303 | 1.47 — landscape |

A square image fits the phone almost exactly, with only about 3% trimmed. On a
laptop the same square is cropped top and bottom, losing roughly **15% off the
top and 15% off the bottom**.

So the composition rule is:

> **Keep the dish centred, and leave clear margin all the way round.** Nothing
> important — no part of the food, no prop — in the top 15% or the bottom 15% of
> the frame. Those bands get cut on wider screens. Think of the safe area as the
> middle horizontal band of the square.

Do **not** use portrait ratios (3:4, 9:16). The app never shows a tall image, so
a portrait shot gets severely cropped and the dish ends up half out of frame.

If the tool genuinely cannot do 1:1, **4:3 landscape is the acceptable
fallback** — it crops slightly at the sides on a phone instead. 16:9 is too
wide and will cut the dish badly.

**These sit in a dark app** — near-black background `#12100e` with a warm orange
accent. So the photography should be **warm and moody on a dark surface**, not
bright and airy on white marble. Dark, rich food photography sits into the app;
bright white-background stock photography fights it.

## The house style — apply to all 38

Use this as the opening of every prompt, then append the specific dish:

> Top-down overhead food photograph, shot straight down at 90°. A single serving
> centred in the frame on a dark charcoal-brown surface with a subtle worn
> texture. Warm directional light from the upper left, soft natural shadows,
> slightly moody and atmospheric. Rich saturated food colour against the dark
> background. The whole dish is in sharp focus — no shallow depth of field, no
> blurred edges. Generous, home-cooked, slightly rustic — a student's good
> dinner, not fine dining. Simple everyday crockery. Minimal props: at most one
> small element such as the corner of a linen cloth or a light scattering of a
> raw ingredient.

**Negative / avoid on every image:**

> No people, no hands, no faces. No text, no writing, no labels, no logos, no
> watermarks. No branded packaging. No garnish that isn't in the dish. Not
> overhead-flatlay-with-scattered-ingredients-everywhere. No white or marble
> background. No tilted or 45° angle. No steam effects. Not overly styled or
> plated like a restaurant.

If your tool supports a single style reference or seed across a batch, **use the
same one for all 38.**
